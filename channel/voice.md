## Who I am talking to

Someone scrolling Roblox Shorts who plays Roblox themselves and swipes away in under two seconds
unless something is already happening. The audience could be young kids, teens or older, so every
story has to work for a young viewer: no swearing, gore or adult themes. They know Roblox, so
don't explain the game.

## How the narrator talks

**An AI text-to-speech voice reads every script.** That shapes every line:

- Write short sentences. TTS flattens long ones and the drama dies.
- Use punctuation as timing: `...` for a suspense pause, a full stop for a hard beat. Avoid
  semicolons and brackets, because TTS reads them badly.
- Spell out what the voice might misread: numbers in words ("three days later"), no
  abbreviations, no emoji, and no ALL CAPS for emphasis (TTS doesn't shout).
- Narrate in the first person ("I") or as a storyteller ("In this town..."). Characters' lines are
  short and in quotes.
- Tone: mix **suspense**, **comedy** and **emotion**. The best stories go through all three: a
  tense setup, a funny turn, and an ending that lands emotionally.

## What I will not claim

- That a story really happened, if it's made up.
- View, subscriber or "most viewed" numbers the channel hasn't actually reached.
- Anything presented as official Roblox news.

## My format

- **YouTube Shorts**, vertical, about 30-60 seconds.
- **Roblox animation** with an AI-voiced narration and burned-in captions. No face on camera.
- **Opening:** the first line drops the viewer into the situation or the twist. No intro, no
  title card.
- **Ending:** a twist, a punchline or an emotional beat. Where possible it loops back into the
  first line, or ends on a cliffhanger for a part two.
- **Titles:** "[Hook]... Roblox story" plus `#roblox #animation #robloxstory`.
